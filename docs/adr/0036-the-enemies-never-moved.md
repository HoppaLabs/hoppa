# 0036 — The enemies never moved

**Status:** accepted (day 17)

`dash/7`. Side-on enemies patrol. They never have, in any build.

## What was reported

Watching a nine-year-old play, live:

> The lizard enemy doesn't move? Can you check this for the other enemy
> characters like goblin and bat, check the side view as well please?

It was not the lizard. `src/core/level.ts` is explicit that an enemy's letter
is cosmetic — a `B`, a `D` and a `G` "walk, patrol, chase, hurt and die exactly
as a `G` does" — so if one of them was stuck, all of them were.

Measured. Every enemy in every shipped room, watched for 300 ticks with the
player standing perfectly still:

| room | view | enemy | moves? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| first steps | above | goblin | 4.0 cells |
| four corners | above | bat, goblin | 4.0 cells each |
| the gauntlet | above | bat, goblin | 4.0 cells each |
| the narrow way | above | bat | 4.0 cells |
| **the tall room** | **side** | **lizard** | **0.0 cells** |

Then in a scratch room, side-on, all three letters and four placements: every
one stuck, 0.00 cells. Then in `dash/1` through `dash/6`: the same code, byte
for byte, in all six. **No enemy has ever moved in the side-on game.** It went
unnoticed for sixteen days because until day 16 no side-on level had an enemy
in it — they were added when the rooms were rebuilt around one-deep platforms.

## Why

Two numbers, thirty-two subcells apart.

A body is a square 96 subcells from its middle to each edge, and it falls until
it will not fit. So a creature standing on the floor of a cell has its middle
at **160** of that cell's 256. An enemy was placed at `cellCentre()` — **128** —
and nothing ever made it fall, so it hung 32 subcells above the ground it was
supposed to be standing on. That is an eighth of a cell: about two pixels at the
size a phone draws this. Nobody was ever going to see it.

The ledge test is what stops a walker strolling off the end of a platform, and
it asked whether there was floor two subcells below the body's feet:

```ts
const cy = toCell((y + BODY + 2) | 0);   // y + 98
```

From 160 that is 258 — the cell below, the floor, correct. From 128 it is 226,
which is still inside the enemy's **own** cell, and that cell is empty because
the enemy is standing in it. So `floorUnder()` returned false, `ledge` was true,
and the walker turned round. Every tick. Of every game. Two of them pivoting on
the spot, thirty times a second, forever.

Both halves had to be wrong at once for it to hide: a wrong resting height that
nothing else read, and a distance-based test that only worked from the right
height.

## What changed

1. An enemy stands where a body rests: `cellCentre(row) + REST`, where
   `REST = (ONE >> 1) - BODY` is 32. They line up with the player now, which
   they never did.
2. The ledge test asks about **the cell below the one the walker is in**, not
   about a distance that happens to work from one exact height. A question
   about a cell cannot be thrown off by a fraction of one.
3. Both the ledge test and the wall test are taken at the **leading edge** of
   the body rather than at its middle. Measured on the middle, a walker put its
   face 0.37 of a cell into the bricks before turning, and stepped the same
   distance out over the end of a platform. Nobody had ever seen that either,
   for the same reason.

Measured after, in a three-cell pen with walls at both ends: the body's edges
reach 5.04 and 7.94 against walls at 5.00 and 8.00. It no longer touches them.

## What it does not fix

A walker on a **one-cell** platform shuffles inside 0.17 of a cell. Its body is
192 subcells wide in a 256-subcell cell, so there is 64 subcells of slack and it
uses it. Left alone deliberately: it is a twitch on a platform with nowhere to
go, and every rule that would remove it makes the ordinary case worse — either
the walker overhangs the ledge again, or it stops dead on platforms that are
perfectly walkable. Two cells and up patrol properly, which is every platform in
every shipped room.

## Hard rule 3

`dash/6` stays, unchanged and still routed. Every link already sent replays with
its enemies rooted to the spot, exactly as the person who sent it played it. The
nine shipped rooms are regenerated onto `dash/7`, which changes their codes —
they are generated by `tools/pack.ts` and were never anybody's permanent link.

`test/dash-v7.test.ts` pins both: that v7 walks and that v6 does not.
