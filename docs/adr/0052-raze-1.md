# 0052 — raze/1: buildings that come down

Day 20. Accepted, and built.

## What was asked for

"The buildings should be destructible and turn into fire when attacked by a
jaeger or kaiju? How could we implement this?" — and, after the options were
put: "Start with raze/1."

## Why a new engine id and not roam/9

It could have been roam/9. Two reasons pulled the same way:

- Hard rule 3 says never change engine behaviour in place. roam/9 would have
  kept that rule — old links pin roam/8 — but it would have made every NEW
  adventure level a level with smashable walls, which is a large design change
  to a game nobody asked to change.
- Hard rule 4 says cosmetics never touch `stateHash()`. So the **city skin
  cannot decide the rules**: a tileset id is presentation, and presentation may
  not reach the engine. The rules have to live somewhere the wire format
  already carries as rules, and the engine id is that place.

`ENGINE_IDS` is four bits and append-only, with ten of sixteen slots free. One
is now `raze`. A city level says `raze/1` and a cave level says `roam/8`, and
neither can turn into the other by being redrawn.

The pair is worth stating plainly: **the city is a skin AND an engine, and
neither decides the other.** A raze level with no skin is a cave you can smash;
a roam level with `tiles=6` is a city you cannot.

## What is different

- A swing smashes the wall directly ahead, **if the creature is strong enough**
  (FORCE 4 of 5). This is the first thing in the game that strength *unlocks*
  rather than merely scales — and it is what finally makes Vance and Bash feel
  different from Pell and Nim without touching the pip budget.
- A smashed wall burns for three seconds, then becomes floor. Burning costs a
  heart the way any fire does. Permanent fire would let a child wall themselves
  into a corner with their own weapon; a timer makes a smashed building a
  shortcut you have to wait for.
- Only the **player** smashes anything. A kaiju that breaks walls can seal a
  player behind a fire they did not light, and the fantasy is that the jaeger
  is the one wrecking the city anyway. If the monsters should do it too, that
  is raze/2 and a fresh set of measurements.

## What is deliberately not different

- Patrols are worked out once, from the walls the level was **drawn** with.
  Smashing a corridor open does not re-route the thing pacing it: a patrol is a
  property of the room a child drew, and a guard whose beat changes under it is
  a guard nobody can plan around.
- A razed wall only ever **adds** somewhere to walk, so a level that passed L3
  and L4 still passes them, and a room the bot could finish it can still
  finish. **The verifier needed no changes and neither did the bot** — which
  was the largest risk on the original proposal, and it evaporated the moment
  destruction became player-only.

## The one real risk, and the three goes it took to test it

A smashed building is state. State that is not hashed is state a replay can
disagree about, and a shared level is only worth anything because its proof
replays cold. So `razed` and `ember` go into `stateHash()`, beside the doused
fires and for exactly the reason written next to them.

Proving that is harder than it looks, and `check:mutants` caught two vacuous
attempts before the third worked:

1. **Replay the same log twice and compare.** Passes with the state deleted
   from the hash entirely — both runs drop it identically.
2. **Swing in one run and not the other.** Passes because `swing` is hashed, so
   the two differ whether or not the razing does.
3. **Both press right into the same wall** — which blocks both, so neither ever
   moves and both end facing the same way — **and only one of them swings.**
   After sixty idle ticks the swing has run out of both. Nothing is left to
   differ except which buildings are down. Plus a control: the same pair with
   the swing taken out lands on the *same* hash.

Only the third fails when the state leaves the hash. The first two were tests
that could not fail, which is the same mistake as the nine-day-old REACH test
that started `tools/mutate.ts` in the first place.
